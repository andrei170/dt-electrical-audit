“National Electrical Contractors” — On A Site Of 755 Words With No Sitemap.

755
words carrying a national commercial claim

The positioning is ambitious and the site behind it is not.

Your headline says “National Electrical Contractors leading the way for more than a decade.” That is a serious claim, aimed at serious buyers.

There is no sitemap, no structured data, and nothing measuring whether any of it works.

Site read 24 September 2026
What Is Already Right
More than most electrical contractors publish
  • A clear commercial and industrial position. Both words appear four times each — you are not pretending to be a domestic sparky, which is the right call.
  • Case Studies. You have a section for them. On commercial work that is the single most persuasive thing on a website, and most contractors have none.
  • An enquiry form, and two tappable phone numbers.
  • A real trading address published — 117A Shakespeare Street, Southport PR8 5AN. Buyers check.
Finding 1 — Nothing Is Measured
Google Analytics not found Google Tag Manager not found Google Ads tag not found Meta pixel not found Call tracking not found
You cannot tell whether the site produces anything

There is no analytics of any kind. No visitor count, no idea which pages get read, no way to know whether an enquiry came from a search, a referral or somebody who already knew you.

On commercial and industrial work the sales cycle is long — somebody researches you weeks before they call. Without analytics that entire research phase is invisible, and it is the part you could influence.

Analytics is free. This is a short job, not a project.

Finding 2 — A National Claim With A Local Footprint Published
Your headline: "National Electrical Contractors" Your address: 117A Shakespeare Street, Southport PR8 5AN Your numbers: 01704 615100 (Southport dialling code) Pages about regions you cover: none found Pages about sectors you serve: none found Published sitemap: none
The claim and the evidence do not currently match

There is nothing wrong with being a Southport firm that works nationally — plenty are. But a buyer in Birmingham or Glasgow reading “national” will look for proof, and what they find is one address and a local phone number.

The fix is not to drop the claim. It is to evidence it — the regions you actually cover, the sectors you work in, and case studies with locations on them.

You already have case studies. If those jobs are spread around the country, that is your proof and it is already sitting there.

Finding 3 — No Accreditations Shown, On Work That Is Gated By Them
NICEIC not found on the site NAPIT not found ECA not found CHAS not found SafeContractor / Constructionline / SSIP not found
This is the highest-value conversation we could have

On commercial and industrial work, main contractors, councils, housing associations and facilities managers routinely require scheme membership before a firm can be added to a tender list at all. Requirements vary by buyer and contract — this is not a rule we can state for your market — but it is why most contractors chasing that work display them prominently.

The word “Compliance” is in your trading name. That makes their absence from the website more noticeable, not less.

If you hold them, this is a twenty-minute fix: logos and registration numbers, on every page, plus insurance cover levels. If you do not, that is the single most important thing to discuss, and it matters more than anything else in this document.

What We Would Do, In Order
  • Settle the accreditation question first. Everything else on this list is worth less than the answer to it.
  • Put analytics on, so the long research phase before a commercial enquiry becomes visible.
  • Build the case studies out — sector, location, scope, and what the problem was. They are the proof of the national claim and you already have the material.
  • Add a page per sector you serve, and per region if you genuinely cover them.
  • Publish a sitemap and add LocalBusiness and Service structured data. One-off technical jobs.
  • Then talk about enquiries — once there is something for a buyer to read and something to measure.

A decade in, a real address, case studies and a national ambition.

The claim is fine. It just needs the evidence published underneath it.
