This is a substantial, well-organised site. The problem is not what is missing — it is where it is buried, and that nothing measures any of it.

You publish at least 45 pages, including seven sector pages and nine compliance pages. That is more structure than most electrical contractors ever build.

But there is no analytics, no tag manager, no structured data and no published sitemap — so none of that work is measured, and the credentials that win tender places are three clicks deep.

A Correction We Owe You, And It Is Substantially In Your Favour
Three findings in the first version of this document were wrong

The version issued on 24 September said your site was 755 words, that you had no sector or region pages, and that no accreditations were shown anywhere. All three were wrong, and we have rewritten the document.

What went wrong: the first read measured your homepage and reported the result as though it described the whole site. Your homepage is 755 words — that figure was correct. Your site is not. Because you publish no sitemap, a shallow read finds very little, and we drew a conclusion from our own failure to look properly rather than from your site.

We re-crawled it properly by following your own navigation, page by page, on 25 September. What is below is measured from 45 pages.

We also want to be straight about a second mistake we caught ourselves. An intermediate check appeared to show you displaying ECA and SMAS membership as well. It did not — that check was matching those letters inside ordinary words like "because". We re-ran it properly and the accurate answer is NAPIT and CHAS only. We would rather tell you that than quietly credit you with two schemes you have not claimed.

Finding 1 — Your Credentials Are On The Wrong Three Pages
NAPIT found on 3 of 45 pages /services/electrical-installation /sectors/social-housing /compliance/emergency-lighting-verification CHAS found on 2 of 45 pages /services/electrical-installation /sectors/industrial Homepage neither About page neither Contact page neither NICEIC / ELECSA / ECA / SafeContractor / Constructionline / SMAS / SSIP not found anywhere on the site
This is the highest-value item in the document and it is a twenty-minute fix

You hold NAPIT and CHAS and you have barely told anyone. They appear on three deep pages that a buyer reaches only if they are already several clicks into your site and looking at one specific service.

The three pages where they do not appear are the three pages that decide whether you get contacted: the homepage someone lands on, the about page they check you out on, and the contact page they read immediately before deciding whether to call.

On commercial and industrial work, main contractors, councils, housing associations and facilities managers routinely require scheme membership before a firm can be added to a tender list at all. Requirements vary by buyer and by contract — that is not a rule we can state for your market — but it is why contractors chasing that work put the logos in the header and the footer of every page.

The word "Compliance" is in your trading name. That makes the homepage absence more noticeable, not less.

Put the logos and registration numbers in the site footer, so they appear on all 45 pages at once, and add your insurance cover levels. If you hold anything beyond NAPIT and CHAS, that belongs there too — we can only report what is published.

Finding 2 — Forty-Five Pages, And Nothing Measuring Any Of Them
Google Analytics 4 none found Universal Analytics none found Google Tag Manager none found Meta pixel none found Call tracking none found Structured data none found
This was true in the first version and it matters far more now that we know the site is this big

When we thought this was a 755-word site, "no analytics" was a small finding. It is not a small finding on a 45-page site. You have built seven sector pages and nine compliance pages, and you cannot tell which of them has ever produced an enquiry.

Most of your enquiries on this kind of work arrive as phone calls, and with no call tracking those are invisible too. So the honest position is that you do not currently know whether your website produces work at all, let alone which part of it.

No structured data means Google is left to infer what you are from your words. For a firm with a clean sector-and-service structure already built, that is a straightforward thing to add and it maps onto pages you already have.

Finding 3 — No Sitemap, On A Site Large Enough To Need One
/sitemap.xml 404 /sitemap_index.xml 404 /robots.txt 200
This is how a 45-page site gets read as a small one

A sitemap is the file that tells a search engine every page you have. Without it, Google finds pages by following links, which means anything buried deep gets found late or not at all.

We know exactly how that fails, because it is what happened to us. Our first read of your site found very little and we wrongly reported it as a small site. A search engine crawling shallowly makes the same mistake, and it does not issue a correction.

This is a generated file, not a writing job — minutes of work, and it is the cheapest item on this list.

What We Would Do, In Order
  • Put NAPIT and CHAS in the site footer, with registration numbers and insurance cover levels. One change, appears on all 45 pages, twenty minutes. This is the highest-return item here.
  • Generate a sitemap. Minutes. Your site is large and well structured and Google is currently finding it the hard way.
  • Install analytics and call tracking. Until this is done, every other decision about the site is guesswork — including whether any of it is worth keeping.
  • Add structured data to the sector and service pages. Your structure is already right; this just tells Google what it is looking at.
  • Put the accreditations on the sector pages too, not just industrial and social housing. A healthcare or education buyer is checking for exactly the same thing.
  • Then look at the case studies. If those jobs are spread around the country, that is the evidence for the national claim in your headline, and it is already sitting there unused.
